## Features

<https://checklist.design/website/features>

A page that walks through the full capabilities of the product, helping prospects understand what it does in depth.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Feature grouping** — Capabilities organised into logical themes or categories so the page is scannable and not a wall of bullet points
  - *Conseil :* Features grouped by the job they help the user accomplish are easier to scan than those organised by how the product is built internally
- [ ] **Feature descriptions** — Each feature explained in one or two sentences from the user's perspective, what they can do, not what the system does
- [ ] **Feature visuals** — Screenshots, GIFs, or short video clips showing each feature in action
  - *Conseil :* For complex interactions, animated GIFs showing a feature in use can provide better storytelling and value than static screenshots
- [ ] **Benefits framing** — Each feature connected explicitly to an outcome and/or benefit, not just the capability
- [ ] **Social proof per feature** — A relevant customer quote placed alongside the feature it relates to, making the case more concrete.
- [ ] **CTA** — A conversion point at the bottom and optionally inline throughout, for visitors who are convinced mid-page

## Blog Post

<https://checklist.design/website/blog-post>

A blog post page displays a single article's content, including its title, author, publication date, and body text. It often includes related media (images, videos), social sharing options, and commenting functionality, allowing readers to engage with the content and navigate to other posts.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Title** — Copy that educates the user on what to expect to read, typically phrased in an eye-catching or interesting manner
- [ ] **Sub heading** — Elaborating on the title, defining the purpose of the post
- [ ] **Thumbnail** — A visual aid to illustrate the themes of the content
  - *Conseil :* Thumbnail style should draw from branding and style guide
- [ ] **Author** — Who wrote and contributed to the post
  - *Conseil :* Make an author's profile selectable to let users follow their favourite writers in the blog
- [ ] **Content** — The actual blog post itself
  - *Conseil :* Consider responsiveness and how content is represented on smaller devices
- [ ] **Social sharing tools** — Ability to share or send the post to other people
  - *Conseil :* Positioning this element in a fixed position on the page allows the opportunity for the user to share the post at any point
- [ ] **Lead conversion content** — Introducing relevant information about your product that ties in with the post
- [ ] **Related articles** — Suggested posts relevant to the current post
- [ ] **Opt-in for subscription** — A signup form for users to join which will subscribe them to a platform that lets you notify them of new posts
- [ ] **Date posted** — When the post was shared live
- [ ] **Tags** — A specific category for users to explore as a topic
- [ ] **Comments** — A section for discussion that users can join and contribute to

## Careers

<https://checklist.design/website/careers>

A careers page typically displays job openings, company culture, and employment opportunities. It allows potential candidates to explore available positions, learn about the organization's values, and typically includes functionality to submit job applications or contact recruiters.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Company story** — Share how the business started off and the journey it has experienced
  - *Conseil :* Don't just share the what, but the why and motivations behind the foundation
- [ ] **Up to date information** — Ensure all the content is up to date, especially quantitative values e.g. team headcount, offices open, time operating
- [ ] **Employee benefits** — Perks and value props for potential employees
  - *Conseil :* Ensure the benefits show real, tangible value and aren't gimmicks
- [ ] **Teams and divisions** — Either a breakdown of the team members or what areas you currently have

  - *Conseil :* Break up the roles by department for easier scanning
- [ ] **Working locations** — Showcase where your offices are, and if you offer remote work
- [ ] **Employee testimonials** — Feature stories from current employees that share why they love working at the company and what someone can expect

A FAQ (frequently asked questions) page provides answers to common user queries in a structured, easy-to-scan format. It serves as a self-service resource to address typical customer concerns, reduce support inquiries, and help users find solutions quickly.

- [ ] **Search functionality** — Keyword search for users to look up their questions

## Privacy

<https://checklist.design/website/legal-privacy>

Page covering the legal terms of using the product (privacy policy, terms of service, and cookie policy) written clearly and kept current.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Privacy policy** — A clear document explaining what data is collected, how it is used, how long it is kept, and how users can request deletion
  - *Conseil :* A plain-language summary at the top is an option, to allow most users understand the policy without diving into deeper details unless necessary
- [ ] **Terms of service** — The agreement governing use of the product (user responsibilities, acceptable use, limitation of liability)
- [ ] **Cookie policy** — A clear explanation of which cookies are used, what they do, and how users can control them
  - *Conseil :* Cookie policies separated from the privacy policy are increasingly what users search for, since they serve different functions and are regulated differently in most jurisdictions
- [ ] **Last updated date** — A clear timestamp on every legal document so users know if they are reading the current version
- [ ] **Version history or changelog** — For products with engaged users, a log of what changed between policy versions — builds trust and reduces enquiries.
  - *Conseil :* A summary of changes in plain English at the top of an updated policy is far more reassuring than just changing the date.
- [ ] **Contact for legal queries** — A dedicated email or address for data, privacy, or legal enquiries as required by most regulations.

## Login

<https://checklist.design/website/login>

A login page is a critical component of many web applications and websites, serving as the gateway for users to access personalized features, secure content, and their own data.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Logo** — Either your complete logo or a symbol mark
- [ ] **Title** — Tell the user this is where they login
- [ ] **Account identification** — A unique identifier for the user
  - *Conseil :* Consider multiple identifiers to login with e.g. username, or email address, or phone number
- [ ] **Password** — A secure, private code that allows access to their account
  - *Conseil :* Create the ability to make the password visible
- [ ] **Link to reset password** — A user may forget their unique code - let them change it!
  - *Conseil :* Position this link close to your password input field
- [ ] **Link to sign up** — A user may not have an account and landed on this page anyway
- [ ] **Login via third party** — Turn text fields into one click by allowing users to connect via a third party e.g. Google, Apple, Facebook, Twitter
- [ ] **Testimonial** — Reinforcing the brand and product never goes out of style
- [ ] **Blog post** — An opportunity to show off content relevant to the user
- [ ] **New feature** — The perfect way to build excitement for something new right before a user is about to log in and see it!

## Security

<https://checklist.design/website/security>

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Certifications and compliance** — Logos and names of security certifications held e.g. SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA
  - *Conseil :* Certification logos should be paired with an explanation of what they cover
- [ ] **Data encryption** — A plain-language explanation of how data is encrypted at rest and in transit
- [ ] **Data residency** — Where customer data is stored and processed, with any regional options available
- [ ] **Access controls** — Who within the company can access customer data, under what conditions, and what controls are in place to limit that access
- [ ] **Vulnerability disclosure** — How security issues are reported and handled e.g. a dedicated email address, a bug bounty programme, or a responsible disclosure policy
- [ ] **Incident history** — A record of past security incidents that were disclosed, with dates and resolution summaries for transparency
- [ ] **Penetration testing** — Details of third-party security audits or penetration tests, with the most recent date and ability to request a report for further details

## Testimonials

<https://checklist.design/website/testimonials>

A page dedicated to social proof, collecting customer quotes, reviews, and success stories in one place.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Customer quotes** — Direct quotes from real customers describing their experience, attributed with name, role, and company
  - *Conseil :* Specific quotes that mention a concrete outcome are far more persuasive than generic praise ('It saved us 6 hours a week' beats 'Love this tool')
- [ ] **Customer details** — Name, role, company, and photo for each testimonial, since anonymised quotes carry much less weight
  - *Conseil :* A recognisable company logo next to a quote multiplies its credibility significantly
- [ ] **Use case variety** — Testimonials representing different customer types, industries, and use cases so visitors can find someone like them
- [ ] **Video testimonials** — Short video clips from customers who are willing to speak on camera, for visitors who find video more convincing
- [ ] **Review platform badges** — Aggregate scores from G2, Trustpilot, Capterra, or similar platforms linking to verified reviews
  - *Conseil :* Third-party review scores carry more weight than self-selected quotes, when scores are strong, lead with them

## Affiliate

<https://checklist.design/website/affiliate>

A page that invites potential affiliates or partners to join a programme, explaining how it works and what they stand to earn.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Programme overview** — A clear explanation of what the affiliate or partner programme involves and who it is designed for
- [ ] **Commission or reward structure** — Exactly what affiliates earn (percentage, flat fee, recurring commission) stated plainly
  - *Conseil :* Ambiguous commission structures are the most common reason potential affiliates leave this page without applying
- [ ] **How it works** — A simple step-by-step breakdown of joining, sharing, and earning
  - *Conseil :* If using a third party to connect, it's helpful to include screenshots for steps
- [ ] **Resources provided** — What materials the company provides (links, banners, copy, tracking) to help affiliates succeed
- [ ] **Social proof** — Testimonials or earnings examples from existing affiliates who are benefiting from the programme
- [ ] **Application or signup CTA** — A clear form or button to join the programme

## Compare

<https://checklist.design/website/compare-page>

A page that positions the product directly against a specific competitor, helping prospects who are evaluating alternatives to make a decision.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Clear positioning statement** — An opening that states plainly how this product differs from the competitor being compared
  - *Conseil :* A compare page that claims superiority on every dimension reads as untrustworthy so include honest acknowledgement of trade-offs for more credibility
- [ ] **Feature comparison table** — A side-by-side table of key features showing what each product includes, with clear tick and cross indicators
  - *Conseil :* This table focused on decision-relevant features 

This table doesn't have to mention every single feature, just the most persuasive and relevant to influencing decision
- [ ] **Key differentiators** — A section expanding on the areas where this product genuinely excels beyond a table comparison
- [ ] **Social proof** — Testimonials or quotes from customers who switched from the competitor being compared
- [ ] **Pricing comparison** — A clear, fair comparison of pricing structures if the difference is a meaningful factor for prospects
  - *Conseil :* Pricing comparisons only work best if there's a genuine advantage e.g. cheaper or more value for money spent
- [ ] **CTA** — A direct prompt to try the product or book a demo

## Status

<https://checklist.design/website/status>

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Component status list** — Each major service or component (API, dashboard, integrations, notifications) shown with its current operational state
  - *Conseil :* A single 'All systems operational' row on its own is less useful than per-component status, since users experiencing an issue with one part of the product need to know if it is on their end or yours
- [ ] **Live incident updates** — During an active incident, timestamped updates posted in sequence (identifying the issue, confirming investigation, and reporting resolution)
  - *Conseil :* The cadence of updates matters more than their length — a brief 'investigating' post every 30 minutes is far more reassuring than silence followed by an explanation
- [ ] **Incident history** — An archive of past incidents with dates, duration, affected components, and a brief post-mortem
- [ ] **Uptime metrics** — Historical uptime percentages per component, typically covering a rolling 90-day window
- [ ] **Scheduled maintenance** — Upcoming planned maintenance windows listed in advance with estimated duration and which components will be affected
  - *Conseil :* Scheduled maintenance announced at least 72 hours in advance (and communicated via the subscription channel) is far better absorbed than a surprise
- [ ] **Subscribe to updates** — An email, SMS, or webhook subscription option so users are notified of incidents without having to check the page themselves
- [ ] **Separate domain** — The status page hosted on a domain independent of the main infrastructure, so it remains accessible when the product itself is down
  - *Conseil :* A status page that goes offline during an outage is the single most common implementation failure. A separate hosting arrangement is the only reliable solution

## Press / Media

<https://checklist.design/website/press-media>

A page providing journalists, analysts, and content creators with the resources they need to cover the company accurately.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Company overview** — A concise, factual description of the company, what it does, when it was founded, and who it serves
  - *Conseil :* A press overview written as a ready-to-use paragraph saves journalists time. The easier it is to lift and paste, the more often it gets used
- [ ] **Press coverage** — A curated list of notable media mentions with publication name, headline, and link

- [ ] **Recent press releases** — Archived official announcements for journalists researching the company's history

## Waitlist

<https://checklist.design/website/waitlist>

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Email capture** — A simple form (typically just an email field) to join the waitlist, with a clear statement of what joining means
- [ ] **What to expect** — What waitlist members will receive (early access, a discount, a specific feature) and a rough timeline for when
  - *Conseil :* Vague waitlists that promise nothing specific generate sign-ups but very low activation when access eventually opens
- [ ] **Social proof** — The total number of people already on the waitlist, displayed to signal demand and reduce the fear of backing something niche
- [ ] **Position indicator** — The user's current position in the queue, shown after sign-up and updated as they move up
  - *Conseil :* Showing a specific position ('You are #847') is more motivating than a generic confirmation because it creates a concrete reference point to improve from
- [ ] **Referral mechanic** — A shareable link that moves the user up the list when others join through it, incentivising organic sharing
  - *Conseil :* Referral waitlists consistently grow faster than static ones. The mechanic works because it aligns the user's interest with the product's growth
- [ ] **Progress toward access** — An indication of how close the product is to general launch or how many spots remain at the current access tier

## Cart

<https://checklist.design/website/cart>

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Item name and image** — Each item shown with its name and image, matching what was displayed on the product page.
- [ ] **Update or remove item** — The ability to adjust quantity, change variants, or remove an item directly from the cart.
- [ ] **Item variations** — Selected variants for each item — size, colour, style, or type — displayed clearly alongside the item.

- [ ] **Checkout button** — A prominent button to proceed to payment when the user is ready to complete their order.
- [ ] **Suggested add-ons** — Related or complementary items suggested based on what is already in the cart.

## Search

<https://checklist.design/website/search>

A search results page displays organized findings based on a user's search query. It presents relevant matches in a scannable view to help users quickly find and navigate to their desired destination.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Search box** — The element a user types in to make a search
  - *Conseil :* A search box should be at the top of the page, styled consistently with other search boxes across your website or product Include a button to click to process search Placeholder text can nudge the user to make a relevant search
- [ ] **Visualisation of results** — Showing the items relevant to the search
  - *Conseil :* Give as much detail as you can to contextualize the results e.g. show the title of the page, a description and potentially a thumbnail Show the number of results Include pagination for long list of results (more than 50) If there are no results, point the user towards making another search or another pathway
- [ ] **Search progress** — A search may not be instant, so consider a loading indicator as the product makes the enquiry
- [ ] **Suggested search items** — Offer a search query to save the user from typing e.g. currently trending, most popular, recently search, autocorrection
- [ ] **Categories and/or filters** — Constraints to help users narrow down their search area
- [ ] **Ordering of search results** — How a user can sort results e.g. best price, most relevant, newest first
- [ ] **Highlighting keywords** — Highlight the words in the search enquiry within the results displayed

## Blog

<https://checklist.design/website/blog>

A blog page aggregates and displays multiple articles or posts in a chronological order, typically showing previews, titles, publication dates, and categories. It provides easy navigation through pagination or infinite scroll, allowing users to browse and discover content.

**Checklist**

- [ ] **Hero** — A clear indicator it's a blog, whether it's for an individual, team or company
- [ ] **Navigation** — A logo, links to visit, a CTA, and potentially a search function
  - *Conseil :* Utilise navigational links to show sub topics
- [ ] **Layout** — How sections and blog posts are structured
  - *Conseil :* Consider what's above the fold on mobile, tablet and desktop Consider blog article order (newest first, trending) A grid layout with multiple posts on a row has more density
- [ ] **Blog post preview** — The visuals and text of a blog post
  - *Conseil :* Typography hierarchy is important here - a user must clearly see the heading first A branded thumbnail sets the mood of the content If you include a description, it should be economical in clearly explaining the blog post
- [ ] **Description** — Summarise what's covered in the blog — shown in the hero
- [ ] **Tags and/or categories** — A topic that can be attached to a blog post for convenient filtering
- [ ] **Search function** — Have lots of posts? Search makes it easier for the user to find the content they desire
- [ ] **Opt-in for subscription** — A signup form for users to complete to make them a subscriber of the blog's content
- [ ] **Sidebar** — This can contain additional links and resources that support the blog posts e.g. search function, popular posts, trending topics
- [ ] **Pagination** — Break up your content into pages after 10-20 items (there are pros and cons on both sides of the pagination vs infinite scroll argument, so go with what suits your product better!)

- [ ] **Testimonial or social proof** — Reinforcing the brand and product never goes out of style
- [ ] **Blog post** — An opportunity to show off some amazing content to convert an unsure lead
